    Overview and problem formulation.DOPER combines synthetic supervision and real observations to adapt an object-specific pose estimator (Figure 2). Given a calibrated RGB image, an approximate CAD model, and ordered object-frame keypoints, we esti- mate object rotation and translation relative to the camera. The CAD appearance and surface details may be incomplete, but metric scale must be known. Keypoint identities and their coordinate frame remain fixed throughout adaptation. Shared visual representation and geometric pose estimation.Following the correspondence- based formulation of DOPE [60], we predict image locations of known object points and recover pose with P𝑛P. Our implementation shares a DINOv3- pretrained ConvNeXt backbone [53] between a full- image branch and an object-crop branch.     We present DOPER, which learns detection and key- points jointly and uses geometric fitting to estimate object pose. The same keypoints support multi-view estimation and heatmap refinement, while synthetic initialization and partial real supervision enable adap- tation. DOPER achieves strong RGB results across four object-pose benchmarks and supports illumina- tion adaptation and temporal pose estimation on SPEED+ and SHIRT. Our HANDAL study further shows that reconstructions from onboarding videos can compensate for missing texture and incomplete CAD geometry. These results leave several challenges open: reduc- ing per-object training and refinement costs, and mak- ing reconstruction-based adaptation independent of annotated poses. Furthermore, a universal metric key- point detector could help streamline object onboard- ing and reduce reliance on object-specific training data
