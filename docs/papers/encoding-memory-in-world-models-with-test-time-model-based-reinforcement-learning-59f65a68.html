The work addresses a clear limitation in model-based RL through a principled, well-ablated approach. The evaluation is comprehensive across multiple baselines and environments, supported by compute-matched controls and external benchmark validations.

The authors explicitly discuss and quantify the computational overhead of performing gradient-based optimization during evaluation episodes, as well as the need for exploration-phase adaptations in future work.

    We introduce TT-MBRL, a novel learning framework for MBRL that takes advantage of test-time observations to learn episode-specific patterns and acquire long-term memory. TT-MBRL learns a unique world model and policy for each evaluation episode. In contrast to training time, where model parameters are shared across all episodes in the replay buffer, this allows the agent to fine- tune its parameters to each individual episode. Analogous to the training procedure, the agent samples mini-batches of trajectories to update its parameters. This strategy acts as a form of regularization, mitigating overfitting to the limited test-time observations, which could otherwise degrade performance.
