Quality reviewed

The manuscript presents a conceptually solid, well-motivated, and thoroughly ablated solution to a specific mathematical ambiguity in semi-supervised DETR architectures. It provides balanced empirical results on an established benchmark without overclaiming.

The authors explicitly disclose limitations, including the restriction to the one-to-many training stage, the lack of a full multi-seed significance study, an unbalanced ablation for the reliability cost alone, and confounding between angle metric shape and scale.

    Semi-supervised object detection has recently been extended to DETR, where oriented boxes introduce an ambiguity that does not arise for axis-aligned boxes: the map from parameters to rectangles is two-to-one, since(c x, cy, w, h, θ)and(cx, cy, h, w, θ+π/2) denote the same rotated rectangle. Losses and matching costs are computed from the parameters, so two boxes that coincide in space can be far apart in the metric that drives training. A correctly lo- calized student box is then penalized by the regression loss, and the inflated matching cost leads the Gaussian mixture model (GMM) fil- ter to discard it.
