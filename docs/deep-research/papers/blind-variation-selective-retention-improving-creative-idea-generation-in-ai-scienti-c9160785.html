    Scientific discovery requires ideas that are original, useful, and non-obvious. Yet AI scientists are typically given as much task information as possible during idea generation, which can anchor generation toward familiar approaches. Inspired by the cognitive framework ofBlind VariationandSelective Retention (BVSR), we propose a discovery framework that separates these two roles of information: it generates proposals under deliberately limited task information to broaden exploration, then learns from proposals previously verified on the same task and their outcomes to retain promising candidates for costly empirical evaluation. We evaluate BVSR across five proposer models and 12 scientific tasks.     We implement selective retention using a language model with a scalar scoring head. Given a task specification S and a candidate x, the model produces a score s=f θ(S, x). For a training run, let T contain its top-5% utility candidates. We include all candidates in T and uniformly sample candidates B from the remainder. We apply a pairwise logistic loss [3], restricted to comparisons between these two groups: LSR = X i∈T X j∈B log(1 + exp[−(si −s j)]).(6) Each comparison can be interpreted as a unit of scientific experience: given two previous proposals, the model learns which one was more worthy of empirical investment. Aggregating many such comparisons trains the selector to exploit past outcomes when screening its future candidates.
