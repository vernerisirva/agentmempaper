    An item is a question or topic q over a fixed cor- pus D. System Two is a generative policy G with history ht at step t. Its action at =G(q, ht) is a search query, an evidence request, or a final answer or ranking. A System One judgment J(u, r)∈ Y takes an objective u and a source region r. The objective may be a local evidence question or the 3 full topic; the region contains original text blocks and their source coordinates. We use Jev's three output types (Almeida, 2026). ANouljudgment returns the probability that re- gion r satisfies a stated property, such as contain- ing relevant evidence. AChoicejudgment returns a distribution over a finite set of options, such as source blocks or reading actions. AScorejudgment returns a distribution over ordinal levels. The spec- ified objective and output type bound each source judgment.

    This work studies the coordination of fast and slow thinking at the level of agent architecture. JevGPT assigns local evidence judgments and research plan- ning to separate components. A natural next step is to integrate these capabilities within a unified model that learns when to make rapid local judg- ments and when to engage in extended reasoning. Joint training could allow the model to adapt this balance to the evolving needs of an investigation and allocate computation accordingly. Our evaluation focuses on question answering and document ranking over fixed corpora. Extend- ing this approach to open-web research and long- form synthesis would help explore how fast and slow thinking can support a broader range of re- search tasks.
