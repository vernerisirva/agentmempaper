DeepQuest provides a comprehensive, technically sound, and empirically verified agent workflow for biomedical discovery. The combination of multi-model LLM evaluation, ablation studies, and external clinical validation on ADNI and AIBL provides substantial scientific rigor.

The authors transparently report that clinical significance scores show smaller gains over baselines, that Top-10 ranking metrics are descriptive due to subset nesting, and that candidate discoveries remain bounded by the retrieved coverage of the reference literature corpora.

    Automating experiments does not necessarily advance scientific understanding. Research agents must identify meaningful knowledge gaps, determine what avail- able data can resolve, and assess whether their findings advance existing knowl- edge. We introduce DeepQuest, a knowledge-gap-driven AI scientist framework for Alzheimer's disease research. DeepQuest transforms unresolved questions from domain reviews into traceable, testable hypotheses and prioritizes them through data feasibility screening and groupwise Elo ranking. It couples itera- tive experimentation with two-level auditing that separates experimental reliability from knowledge contribution relative to two frozen literature corpora.     The literature evidence repository provides a traceable reference for comparing experimental conclu- sions against prior research.We decompose clinical studies on AD and related topics into individual finding entries to construct the evidence repositoryKwhere each entrye∈Krecords the core claim, boundary and excerpt. This repository defines the literature scope against which subsequent knowledge contribution is assessed. Evidence sufficiency is assessed before literature comparison to distinguish interpretable conclu- sions from insufficiently supported results. Results are labeled as Uncertain when valid evidence is lacking because of inadequate statistical power, unstable estimates, nonestimable parameters, or incomplete analysis. Conclusions supported by evidence, including directional associations, condi- tional differences, or null effects, proceed to literature comparison.
