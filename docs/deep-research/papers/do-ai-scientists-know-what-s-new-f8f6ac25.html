    We introduce CLAIMNOV, an interpretable, claim- level novelty module for research proposals that can be dropped directly into any AI for scientific discovery pipeline with a novelty check. As shown in Figure 2, CLAIMNOVextracts typed claims from research proposals, retrieves relevant same- dimension claims from a pool of claims extracted from prior art, scores the entailment per extracted claim, aggregates into a novelty score, and passes the full claim profile to a verdict judge for a binary novel or non-novel decision. For validation experi- ments, see Appendix O, and for exact prompts, see Appendix C.

    During the claim extraction stage, CLAIMNOV takes the title and description from the research proposal P and produces, with an LLM (gpt-4o- mini set to temperature 0), a list of contribution claimsC P ={c 1, . . . , cm}. Each claim ci is typed with a dimension dc and an attribute-value pair (ca, cv). Dimensions are drawn from {Problem, Methodology, Resource, Result}, while an attribute represents the axis along which two works can be compared. For instance, for a Result claim, the attribute may be classifica- tion accuracy on CIFAR-10, and the value may be an improvement from 30.1% to 76.9%. We aim to extract a small number of claims per proposal (2.4– 2.7 on average, the prompt allowing 1–4), based on conventions in AI research. We process each paper in the reference pool Π through this stage once, using its abstract as the description text, to produce pool claimsC Π.

    During the claim retrieval stage, CLAIMNOVtakes a claim c from the previous stage's list of claims, embeds its text together with its paper's title with text-embedding-3-large, and produces a set of relevant pool claims R(c) for comparison to that claim. To obtain such a set, we retrieve the k-nearest (by cosine similarity) pool claims of the same di- mension, keeping at most one claim per pool paper. We determine k by pool size, taking k=10 as the minimum based on an independent retrieval valida- tion (see Appendix O), and for larger pools we use the heuristic k= 0.2n d, where nd is the number of pool papers with a claim in that dimension, capped at 40. This gives k=10 for the disguise pools and k≈35for the anchor pools (§4.4).

    During the claim entailment stage, CLAIMNOV judges the entailment of every pair (c, c′) for c′ ∈ R(c) with an LLM (gpt-4o-mini at temperature 0), giving titles and abstracts of pool papers as context. The judge rates the extent to which the pool claim entails or covers the proposal claim on an ordinal scale ℓ(c, c′)∈ {1, . . . ,5}: 1 unrelated, 2 shared topic only, 3 partial coverage with impor- tant details differing, 4 strong coverage with minor details missing, and 5 full coverage of the claim's substantive content. We map these levels onto the unit interval by taking e(c, c′) = (ℓ(c, c′)−1)/4∈ [0,1].
