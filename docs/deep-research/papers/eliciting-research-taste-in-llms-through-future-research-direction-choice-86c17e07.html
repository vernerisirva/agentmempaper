    Canonical source evidence
    Research taste is increasingly discussed in the context of AI scientists, yet re- mains little studied or measured in LLMs. This judgment matters especially in open-ended research, where a direction must be chosen before a verifi- able objective is available. We operationalize research taste through one task of the scientific workflow: choosing which future research direction to pursue given a research goal and current results. Rather than predicting downstream scientometric metrics or treating author choices as objectively correct, we evaluate models against human-authored future-work direc- tions and report author alignment, stable divergence, and unstable choice as behavioral measures of research-direction preference. We construct an order-controlled evaluation of 80 pairwise comparisons from 19 ML papers published between December 2025 and February 2026 across four subdo- mains.

    Construct Validity & Interpretation. Ground truth reflects the author's preference, not an objectively optimal research direction. Distractors are real future works from related papers and could genuinely be better directions. We therefore frame results as author alignment rather than accuracy, and focus on characterizing taste profiles rather than evaluating correctness or recurring failure modes. Post-cutoff contamination also cannot be ruled out entirely, especially for API-accessed models whose training data or retrieval access may extend beyond stated cutoff dates. Future versions should ground human research-direction preferences in richer traces beyond future-work statements, such as author-specific citation trees and expert-sourced steering trajectories over research directions.
