STARLI | FLAGSHIP 13 FISSION Ten Research Design Monographs | Flagships 141-150 / 300 Author: 33 | White Rainbow Epoch | Research Design Working Edition v1.2 This ten-volume STARLI series examines trustworthy AI-assisted micro/nanorobotic experiments and nano-quantum measurement systems. The common research question is how to bind physical observation, decision authority, calibration uncertainty, and independently reconstructable evidence without mistaking computational confidence for experimental proof. Each volume is a 777-page English, nonclinical prospective research design containing 30 mathematical research plates, 82 chapters, 410 separately paginated research studies, 200 Research Gates with six mechanistic annotations, and 33 Phoenix Answer Embryos. Across the ten volumes, the collection contains 300 research plates, 820 chapters, 4,100 study pages, 2,000 Research Gates, and 330 Phoenix candidates. Research scope by flagship F141 | Microrobotic Autonomous Control Authority Envelope at the Limit — Defines physical authority envelopes for microrobotic control, including credential expiry, actuator veto, permission transfer, and independently observed safe stopping. F142 | AI Nano-Quantum System Trustworthy Refusal Decision at the Limit — Studies when an AI-guided nano-quantum system must refuse action, separating calibrated abstention from indiscriminate rejection and unsafe confidence. F143 | Microscale Autonomous Experimental Design at the Limit — Examines autonomous microscale experimental design under specimen, information, energy, and permission budgets, with causal identification and independent replication. F144 | AI Quantum Sensing Uncertainty Calibration at the Limit — Investigates calibration of quantum-sensing uncertainty, including shared drift, covariance, phase ambiguity, photon budgets, and fair classical comparisons. F145 | Nanorobotic Closed-Loop Control Replayability at the Limit — Tests whether nanorobotic closed-loop decisions can be replayed from raw sensor data, actuator histories, calibration versions, and unselected failures. F146 | AI-Driven Microscale Active Sampling at the Limit — Designs AI-driven active sampling experiments that jointly measure analyte recovery, physical disturbance, resource cost, and contamination under blinded controls. F147 | Nano-Quantum System Human Review Mechanism at the Limit — Evaluates human review of nano-quantum decisions through signed authority, contradictory permissions, case-level uncertainty, and independent physical vetoes. F148 | AI Autonomous Research Agent Safety Boundary at the Limit — Defines safety boundaries for autonomous research agents across instrument access, sample authorization, unsafe tool composition, and immutable evidence. F149 | Microrobotic Autonomous System Interlaboratory Generalization at the Limit — Tests transfer of frozen microrobotic protocols between laboratories with different geometries, materials, calibration trees, operators, and failure environments. F150 | AI Nano-Quantum Trustworthy Autonomous Laboratory at the Limit — Integrates sample custody, microrobotic actuation, quantum/classical readouts, controlled autonomy, human oversight, and cross-site evidence reconstruction. Scientific boundary and publication status These books provide falsifiable research questions, candidate mathematical models, prospective blinded tests, competing explanations, negative-result criteria, and successor research interfaces. They do not report performed experiments or establish clinical efficacy, autonomous deployment safety, or quantum advantage. Independent expert verification of physical assumptions, formula applicability, all bibliographic relationships, and the scientific independence of closely related designs remains necessary.
