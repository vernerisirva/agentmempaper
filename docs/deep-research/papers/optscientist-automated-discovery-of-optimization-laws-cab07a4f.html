    Benchmark design.To evaluate whether OptScientist can recover symbolic optimization laws, we constructOptFeynman, a benchmark derived from the AI Feynman equation collection (Udrescu & Tegmark, 2020). The original benchmark asks whether a method can recover an input–output equation. OptFeynman poses a different question: whether a method can recover the symbolic optimization problem that generates observed outcomes. Each analytic equation is therefore treated as a latent objective landscape, and the observed data are sampled as noisy outcomes of this landscape. By Debreu's continuous utility representation theorem, preferences can be represented by a utility function when the preference relation is complete, transitive, and continuous (Debreu et al., 1953). Theorem 5.1 (Continuous utility representation, Debreu) Let Z ⊆Rn, and let ⪰ be a complete, transitive, and continuous preference relation on Z. Then there exists a continuous function u:Z → Rsuch that, for allz 1, z2 ∈ Z, z1 ⪰z 2 ⇐ ⇒u(z 1)≥u(z 2). This result motivates our evaluation: an equivalent optimization problem should preserve the prefer- ence structure induced by the latent objective, while also explaining its stochastic realizations. Limitations and Future Research.When the structure of ground-truth models deviates substantially from an optimization formulation, our approach is likely to fail. Although, mathematically, a broad class of laws not originally formulated as optimization problems can be represented in optimization form, the OptFeynman results show that OptScientist can recover the original laws from such representations. Future research is needed to characterize the limits of the optimization assumption and identify the classes of models that remain discoverable within a feasible computational budget. 15
