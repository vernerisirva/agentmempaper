    Precise mapping of surface deformation is essential for seismic hazard assessment and tectonic process interpretation. Differential Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar (DInSAR) is a established geodetic technique for detecting ground displacement; however, conventional classifiers and standard deep learning models often struggle to resolve subtle deformation signals from complex phase noise and decorrelation artifacts. Focusing on the 6.1 Mw Sındırgı earthquake (Balıkesir, Türkiye), Sentinel-1A DInSAR interferograms were processed to evaluate model performance in a real- world seismic scenario.     To address the limitations of standard ViTs, we propose the INRA-Transformer, a hybrid model that integrates convolutional layers into the Transformer framework. The architecture is composed of three main components designed to handle the noise and scale complexity of the Sındırgı earthquake dataset: Inception-Based Patch Embedding (Multi-Scale Stem):Standard ViTs use a single-scale linear projection to divide the image into patches. However, seismic signals exist at multiple spatial scales. Our model replaces the linear projection with an Inception-style stem (Zhou et al., 2025). By using parallel convolutional filters (e.g., 3x3, 5x5, 7x7), the model can capture micro-scale phase transitions while simultaneously identifying broad deformation trends.
