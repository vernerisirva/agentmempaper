Quality reviewed

The study offers a well-scoped, rigorously conducted benchmark addressing an established open question regarding human-in-the-loop data efficiency in computational pathology, providing reproducible experimental details and clear boundaries.

The authors transparently acknowledge limitations, including restriction to renal pathology, a single 185-patch hold-out test set, predefined non-tuned oversampling weights, and reliance on single training runs without stochastic variance bounds.

    The performance of all model configurations and their fine-tuned variants was evaluated using the F1 score. 21 Predicted and reference instances were matched using an IoU threshold of 0.5. The F1 score was computed for each test patch and then averaged across all test patches. It was calculated as: F1 = 2TP 2TP + FP + FN, where TP, FP, and FN denote true-positive, false-positive, and false-negative nuclei instances, respectively. The F1 score was used as the primary evaluation metric because it jointly accounts for false-positive and false- negative predictions.
