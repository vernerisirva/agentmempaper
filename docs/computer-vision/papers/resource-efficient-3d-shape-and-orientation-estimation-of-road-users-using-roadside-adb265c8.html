Abstract / summary

End-to-end deep learning pipelines for camera-radar 3D object detection are well established in driver assistance systems and autonomous vehicles; however, adapting them to roadside-mounted infrastructure presents significant challenges. Quality reviewed

The manuscript presents a clear, well-scoped engineering contribution with sound technical formulations, complete calibration details, thorough ablation studies, and objective benchmarking on an independent holdout set against ground truth.

The authors explicitly note that evaluation was conducted at a single roadside installation, fixed template bounds limit adaptability across vehicle sub-types, and yaw prediction models are site-optimized.

CONCLUSION This paper presented a low-cost, resource-efficient two-stage framework for real-time 3D shape and orientation estimation of pedestrians, bicycles, and cars using roadside camera-radar setups. The methodology leverages associated sparse radar point clouds to establish precise 3D target positions, applies bounded dimension templates to stabilize object scale against point sparsity, and employs a lightweight neural network to regress site-specific yaw angles

Comprehensive ablation studies established optimal dimension parameters, while comparative evaluations against camera-only homography baselines demonstrated significant performance gains. Operating at25FPS on an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin, the proposed system provides a practical, edge- friendly solution for real-time roadside perception. Despite its efficiency, this work is limited to a single roadside setup, and fixed template bounds cannot capture fine- grained size variations across vehicle sub-classes. Future work will evaluate cross-site transferability across diverse sensor ge- ometries and road geographies, implement dynamic templates, and expand support to heavy vehicles and motorbikes.
