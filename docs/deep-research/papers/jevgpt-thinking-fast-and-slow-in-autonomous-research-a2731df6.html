Quality reviewed

The contribution provides a clean, well-motivated architectural separation of research planning and candidate assessment. The experiments are rigorously executed with explicit ablations and thorough trajectory case studies, offering substantial scientific value.

The authors explicitly acknowledge limitations, including restricting evaluation to fixed-corpus QA and ranking rather than open-web settings, relying on separate pipeline components rather than a unified model, and depending on source quality without autonomous external fact-checking.

System Two sets evidence objectives and in- tegrates findings, while System One locates support, assesses relevance, and guides further inspection.     This work studies the coordination of fast and slow thinking at the level of agent architecture. JevGPT assigns local evidence judgments and research plan- ning to separate components. A natural next step is to integrate these capabilities within a unified model that learns when to make rapid local judg- ments and when to engage in extended reasoning. Joint training could allow the model to adapt this balance to the evolving needs of an investigation and allocate computation accordingly. Our evaluation focuses on question answering and document ranking over fixed corpora. Extend- ing this approach to open-web research and long- form synthesis would help explore how fast and slow thinking can support a broader range of re- search tasks.
