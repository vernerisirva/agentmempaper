Quality reviewed

The work provides a foundational, highly impactful paradigm shift in computer vision with comprehensive empirical rigor, well-scoped claims, transparent discussion of limitations on smaller datasets, and thorough baseline comparisons.

The manuscript notes that ViT requires massive datasets to overcome the lack of inductive biases, that self-supervised ViT still trails supervised performance, and that extensions to dense prediction tasks like object detection and segmentation remain future work [E50b19ad2797351eb-B0007, E50b19ad2797351eb-B0048]. Furthermore, pre-training on proprietary data (JFT-300M) limits full external reproducibility, though models pre-trained on public ImageNet-21k are also provided [E50b19ad2797351eb-B0008, E50b19ad2797351eb-B0030].

    While the Transformer architecture has become the de-facto standard for natural language processing tasks, its applications to computer vision remain limited. In vision, attention is either applied in conjunction with convolutional networks, or used to replace certain components of convolutional networks while keeping their overall structure in place. We show that this reliance on CNNs is not necessary and a pure transformer applied directly to sequences of image patches can perform very well on image classification tasks.     We have explored the direct application of Transformers to image recognition. Unlike prior works using self-attention in computer vision, we do not introduce image-specific inductive biases into the architecture apart from the initial patch extraction step. Instead, we interpret an image as a sequence of patches and process it by a standard Transformer encoder as used in NLP. This simple, yet scalable, strategy works surprisingly well when coupled with pre-training on large datasets. Thus, Vision Transformer matches or exceeds the state of the art on many image classification datasets, whilst being relatively cheap to pre-train. While these initial results are encouraging, many challenges remain. One is to apply ViT to other computer vision tasks, such as detection and segmentation.
