AI assessment inconclusive

While the methodological concept is clearly presented, the empirical evaluation contains critical confounding factors: SWAM was trained with knowledge distillation and teacher-initialized weights while the comparison baseline was not [Ef61cc48d32462636-B0025, Ef61cc48d32462636-B0037]. With performance margins being very small (often under 0.2%), this training mismatch introduces substantial uncertainty regarding the actual efficacy of the merging algorithm.

The authors acknowledge that evaluation is limited to DeiT-Small, that standard full ImageNet-1K was not evaluated due to hardware constraints, and that training recipes differed regarding distillation and head initialization [Ef61cc48d32462636-B0037].

CONCLUSION In this work, we introduced SWAM, a training-based token merging framework that incorporates multiplicative maximum-saliency pair ranking and asymmetric saliency- weighted aggregation. The two components provide comple- mentary mechanisms for regulating token selection and preserv- ing informative representations during progressive reduction.
