Quality reviewed

The manuscript presents sound theoretical and empirical justifications for departing from standard MSE-based PTQ objectives in spiking networks, executes a comprehensive set of controlled experiments, and clearly details all implementation aspects.

The authors observe performance degradation when jointly compressing to 2-bit on complex tasks due to accumulating flipped spikes, and note that pre-fire quantization under soft reset requires a wider grid spanning above the threshold.

    On neuromorphic chips, spiking neural networks (SNNs) consume far less energy than artificial neural networks (ANNs), but the limited memory of these chips requires low-bit synaptic weights and membrane potentials. Exist- ing post-training quantization (PTQ) methods provide them without retraining and, like those for ANNs, minimize nu- merical errors such as the mean squared error (MSE). In an SNN, however, a quantization error reaches later layers only through the spikes it flips. We find that errors of equal MSE can cause very different accuracy losses, whereas er- rors that flip the same fraction of spikes cause the same loss. We therefore proposeSpikeQ, a spike-guided PTQ frame- work. For weights, we select the level of each weight by simulating the downstream neurons and minimizing their spike errors.
