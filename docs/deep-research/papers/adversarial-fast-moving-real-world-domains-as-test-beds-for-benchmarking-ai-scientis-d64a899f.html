Its negative and nuanced empirical results are thoroughly grounded by objective baselines, statistical significance tests, and detailed qualitative error analyses.

The authors candidly acknowledge that matching real innovations does not prove internal reasoning parity, expert outputs form an incomplete ground truth, proxy domains differ from pure scientific research, and human review was conducted by a non-expert single author [E67304abafdac3dc0-B0043, E67304abafdac3dc0-B0063].

    Our framework evaluates AI scientist capabilities through a time-delayed generation task. For each domain, we define an information cutoff t and construct a fixed input corpus containing only material available before that point, such as rules, constraints, public context, and relevant prior ex- amples. The AI system is then asked to generate structured candidate artifacts from this corpus, such as ideas, designs, or solutions. These generations are evaluated against post- cutoff artifacts produced by real-world experts. This cre- ates a prospective-style evaluation of whether models can generate non-trivial ideas under realistic constraints, while removing the risk of information leakage.     Novelty matching in the F1 domain utilized three stages. First, generated ideas and real innovations were embedded and shortlisted using a dual top-k retrieval strategy (k= 3), retaining the nearest real innovations for each generated idea and the nearest generated ideas for each real innovation. Shortlisted pairs were assessed by an LLM judge (GPT-5.5) for MATCH, PARTIAL, or NO MATCH. Candidate matches then underwent human review to verify if the idea described a similar physical effect to the real innovation. In parallel, each generated idea was assessed along three quality dimen- sions: citation accuracy, rule compliance, and engineering plausibility. An idea was treated as passing these quality fil- ters only if its cited regulations were substantively accurate, its design was legal or plausibly within a regulatory grey zone, and its mechanism was physically plausible.
