Quality reviewed

The paper provides an adequately scoped, well-formulated, and thoroughly ablated contribution to knowledge graph RAG. The evaluation relies on standardized objective benchmarks with clear comparative baselines and transparently documents both strengths and task-specific weaknesses.

The manuscript explicitly discusses limitations, including weaker performance on narrative multi-hop tasks relative to full-corpus or agentic iterative search, performance degradation under growing distractor volumes in EventQA, and reliance on fixed heuristic diffusion hyperparameters.

    Motivation.Semantic retrieval scores graph items independently. It can therefore re- trieve two strongly matching facts while missing the relation that connects them, simply because the connecting relation is not itself similar to the query. Unrestricted graph ex- pansion has the opposite problem: high-degree entities introduce many generic facts and consume the context budget. ActionRAG treats the initial retrieval as a proposal rather than a final result. It constructs a bounded query-local graph, retroevaluates each proposed item using nearby evidence, removes weak proposals, and exposes useful relations that were absent from the initial set.     ActionRAG combines reconciled graph construction with query-local receiver-normalized diffusion, pruning, and materialization for long-term agent memory. Across eight hetero- geneous deterministic MAB columns, it records the highest reported retrieval-based score on six and an unweighted mean of 55.1. Component ablations indicate that the best con- figuration combines reconciled, summarized indexing with retrieval-time refinement. Gains center on test-time learning and conflict resolution where weaker MH-DocQA and Detec- tiveQA results expose limits for narrative evidence. It also maintains low query cost and sub-half-second main-evaluation retrieval, while packed context remains nearly constant as the graph grows. Future work can include better algorithms for graph expansion which are more robust to hyperparameter values.
