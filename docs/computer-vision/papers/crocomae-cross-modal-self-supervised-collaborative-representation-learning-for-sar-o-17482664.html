Abstract / summary

Remote sensing increasingly captures the Earth's surface through complementary sensors such as SAR and optical, yet ground-truth annotations remain scarce, limiting supervised approaches. Quality reviewed

The work provides a clear and conceptually well-motivated contribution to cross-modal representation learning in remote sensing, backed by rigorous experimental controls and baselines evaluated across multiple runs with reporting of variance. The empirical evaluation and ablations solidly substantiate the claims.

The pre-training requires separate forward passes for augmented and masked images, leading to higher GPU compute time compared to single-pass designs like CROMA. The evaluation is focused on Sentinel-1 SAR and Sentinel-2 optical data without validation yet on other modality pairs.

Publication: preprint. arXiv manuscript identifier; no external publication established by the available metadata.

METHOD CroCoMAEis built on the Vision Transformer (ViT) archi- tecture and combines masked autoencoding with contrastive learning at two complementary levels of representation. At the image level, the CLS token is trained with an intra-modal con- trastive loss to produce globally discriminative, augmentation- invariant representations. At the patch level, the embedding of each per-modality encoder is split into a shared subspace, aligned across sensors, and an unshared subspace, left free to retain complementary, possibly modality-specific information.
