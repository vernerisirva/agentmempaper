Quality reviewed

The study offers a rigorous, well-motivated, and novel framing of speaker attribution in conversational memory, accompanied by extensive empirical analysis across real longitudinal datasets and realistic conversational stress tests. The experiments are methodologically transparent and properly controlled.

The manuscript comprehensively documents limitations, noting that LoCoMo-ID uses synthetic voices rather than spontaneous overlapping multi-party recordings, that condition shifts rely on acoustic simulations, and that the identity-gated access protocol serves as an experimental proxy rather than a production biometric security system.

For each spoken turn, an audio LLM (Xu et al., 2025b) generates a transcript, while a speaker- embedding model extracts acoustic identity evi- dence. The router (Section 2.2) assigns the turn to an existing local entity or creates a new one. Each entity thus maintains acoustic state and user-attributed textual memory, while tran- script semantics do not influence acoustic routing or center updates.
