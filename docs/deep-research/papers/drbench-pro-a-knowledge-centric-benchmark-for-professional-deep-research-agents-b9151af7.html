Abstract / summary

Deep research agents gather information from multiple sources and synthesize it into reports on complex, open-ended questions. Existing benchmarks assess report quality along several dimensions, but lack a dedicated knowledge-centric evaluation of which research directions a report covers and how deeply it explores...

Quality reviewed

The study presents a rigorously formulated and thoroughly validated benchmark contribution with substantial scope, clear mathematical definitions, and extensive human-grounded calibration. The experimental controls and evaluations are exemplary.

The evaluation relies on Wikipedia-derived knowledge structures which may favor certain entity types, and automated judge scoring requires careful thresholding, though validated extensively against human annotations.

    A reference space for knowledge coverage needs to connect research topics with supporting evidence. Wikipedia provides linked entities whose relationships can be examined in their source context. To span diverse domains, we adopt all 24 topic categories from WebOrganizer (Wettig et al., 2025) and select two root entities per category. Each root has a sufficiently detailed, reliably parseable article and enough outgoing links to support expansion. Starting from each root, we expand Wikipedia links breadth-first. Nodes represent entities, and edges represent relationships supported by link anchors and surrounding text. We organize the retained expansion as a rooted tree, with one parent per non-root node and a unique path from the root.     A full knowledge tree spans more material than a single research request can reasonably target. We therefore sample smaller subgraphs, each defining the scope of one research case. Each case retains the root and 10 complete branches, preserving the knowledge structure within the selected directions. To make each scope coherent, Claude Opus 4.8 jointly selects three groups of branches from each tree, using the candidate entities' page summaries, relations to the root, and descendant information.     Annotators and annotation procedure.The annotations support the rubric-level human–judge comparison in Section 4.5. Ten PhD-level annotators participated in the human evaluation. The annotation sample comprised 3,695 report–rubric pairs from 12 reports, with each report representing a different benchmark domain.     The report-level assessment in Section 4.5 comprises 72 reports from six deep research agents across 12 cases from distinct domains. Each case includes six reports answering the same research query. Ten PhD- level annotators participate in this assessment. For each case, the same three annotators from this pool independently rate all six reports on breadth, depth, and overall quality using a 1–10 integer scale, and their mean ratings serve as the human reference. Annotators assess reports without access to agent identities, automatically computed scores, or one another's ratings. Differences among annotators are reflected in the mean rating.
