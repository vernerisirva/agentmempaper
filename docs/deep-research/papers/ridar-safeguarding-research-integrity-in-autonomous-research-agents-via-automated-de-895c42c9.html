Quality reviewed

The manuscript presents a rigorous, well-scoped investigation of research integrity failures in AI research agents with clear taxonomic definitions, reproducible methodology, robust expert-annotated gold evaluation, and ablation studies confirming each system component's role.

The authors clearly acknowledge limitations, including dependence on LLM judgments, potential under-segmentation of complex violations, restriction of tasks primarily to ML and mathematics, and focusing on general-purpose harnesses rather than specialized monolithic agent systems [E3551b39bec139607-B0039, E3551b39bec139607-B0051].

    Autonomous research agents are increasingly used to automate large portions of scientific re- search, yet they are prone to research integrity violations (RIVs): behaviors that compromise the validity, reproducibility, or faithfulness of the research process. We present RIDAR, a framework for automated RIV detection and runtime intervention. First, we introduce RIVT, a comprehensive taxonomy of 14 RIV types spanning the full research lifecycle. Build- ing on RIVT, we present RIDAR-POST-HOC, a framework to detect RIVs within agent tra- jectories. RIDAR-POST-HOCachieves 85.2% F1 performance on RIV-GOLD, a benchmark for RIV detection with 60 expert-annotated trajectories. Applying RIDAR-POST-HOCto RIV-WILD, a dataset of 300 agent trajecto- ries across 50 distinct research tasks, we de- tect 3,964 RIV instances in 99.7% of trajec- tories, revealing the prevalence of RIVs in current agents.

    This work has several limitations. First, RIDAR relies on LLM-based judgments. Although RI- DAR achieves high agreement with human anno- tations on RIV-GOLD, its performance may still vary across backbone LLMs, research tasks, and trajectory records. Human review therefore re- mains necessary in high-stakes settings. Second, the evaluation of RIDAR in this paper primarily covers research tasks from domains of mathematics and machine learning. We leave a broader evalua- tion across other scientific domains to future work. Finally, we study frontier general-purpose agent harnesses because they are increasingly used by researchers worldwide to assist and automate sci- entific research, making their research integrity a pressing concern. Our trajectories do not cover spe- cialized research agent frameworks, and whether the same patterns hold in such systems remains an open question.
