Quality reviewed

The work provides a well-motivated, scalable solution to label-free coreset selection backed by rigorous empirical analysis of activation distributions across 5,265 historical runs, clear algorithmic formulations, and comprehensive downstream cross-domain evaluations.

The authors openly address that optimal sampling strategies (fidelity vs. coverage) vary depending on dataset tail characteristics, and note that fixed retention ratios or redundant modalities (e.g., manipulation proprioception) do not uniformly improve performance.

Coreset estimation methods address this problem by identifying the best data subsets to train models on. However, existing approaches often require proxy model training, extensive annotations, or task-specific tailoring. Unsupervised alternatives typically exploit geometric properties in foundation model feature spaces, but are limited to pairwise image-to-image comparisons or comparisons against a fixed subset. We introduce CoreDNA, an unsupervised, task-agnostic coreset estimation approach that pools neuron activations from vision foundation models into per-image and per-dataset distributions.     Given our prior analysis of where the hardest samples lie and whether fidelity or coverage can predict it, we experiment with two formulations: a fidelity-based one that matches the data distribution via the sliced Wasserstein distance, and a coverage-based one that spreads the coreset over the data via farthest-point sampling (FPS).
