Quality pending

The paper is a position/conceptual argument for making a theory of scientific value foundational to AI-for-science. It develops a substantive conceptual framework with formal illustrations (Bell-test loophole closure, Blackwell comparisons, potential-based rewards) and engages alternative views, but it does not empirically validate the proposed theory or demonstrate that the framework can be operationalized in AI systems. The central claims are argued rather than tested, and the paper explicitly frames the work as a research agenda, so the contribution is plausible and well-articulated but not yet established.

The manuscript is a preprint and the extracted text appears to omit some sections or tables (e.g., Table 1 is truncated, some section transitions are abrupt), so the assessment may not reflect the complete argument. Because the paper is a position piece, the absence of empirical validation is expected, but the strength of the contribution depends on whether the proposed formal apparatus can be made checkable and useful in practice, which the paper only sketches.
