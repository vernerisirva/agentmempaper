    Deep research agents answer complex ques- tions through repeated search. They rely on a harness that runs their tool calls and re- turns evidence to their context. This cre- ates a trade-off: Search works best over long passages, which give enough context for re- liable semantic matching. But the returned evidence should be short, because every ob- servation stays in the agent's context for the rest of the trajectory. We introduceCORE, a training-free harness that combines coarse-to- fine search with context management. CORE retrieves passages with a dense retriever. It then reuses the token-level similarities from a late-interaction reranker to return only the most relevant snippet from each passage.     Our experiments examine multi-step information seeking on three benchmarks with fixed text col- lections and reference answers. We chose this set- ting on purpose because it allows controlled, re- producible comparisons across harnesses. Fixed corpora do not capture the dynamic and hetero- geneous nature of the open web, and reference- answer-based evaluation may not fully reflect the quality of long-form synthesis in less constrained research tasks. Evaluating on live web search and open-ended research reports is beyond the scope of this work. We leave it to future research, which could test whether our findings carry over beyond fixed-corpus question answering.
