Evidence

  • The paper introduces conditional memory as a complementary sparsity axis and instantiates it via Engram, a module that modernizes N-gram embedding for O(1) lookup. (pages 2–3)
  • Engram provides a distinct scalable axis of sparse capacity that complements MoE, as shown by a U-shaped allocation law and improved performance over an iso-parameter and iso-FLOPs MoE baseline. (pages 8, 11)
  • The paper uses controlled iso-parameter and iso-FLOPs comparisons, fixed sparsity ratios, and identical training pipelines to isolate the effect of Engram allocation. (pages 7, 9)
  • The evaluation spans language modeling, knowledge, reasoning, reading comprehension, code, math, long-context retrieval, ablations, mechanistic analysis, and system throughput. (pages 9, 11, 18)
  • The central claim that Engram improves over an iso-parameter and iso-FLOPs MoE baseline is directly supported by benchmark results showing consistent gains. (pages 9, 11)
  • The paper positions Engram against N-gram embedding scaling, high-cardinality categorical embeddings, MoE, memory networks, and knowledge storage mechanisms, and identifies fairness and system co-design gaps. (pages 19–20)
  • The paper does not provide a dedicated limitations section and reports no statistical significance or multi-seed analysis for the benchmark gains. (pages 11, 16)
  • The manuscript is well organized, with clear formal definitions, tables, figures, and appendices supporting the main text. (pages 7, 8, 15)
