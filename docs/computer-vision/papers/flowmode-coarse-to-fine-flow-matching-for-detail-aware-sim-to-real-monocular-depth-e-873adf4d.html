Abstract / summary

Monocular depth estimation is a fundamental capability for 3D spatial perception in robotic and intelligent systems. While data-driven discriminative methods have achieved compelling performance for monocular depth estimation, generative approaches can reconstruct fine structural details with stronger generalization...

Quality reviewed

The manuscript presents a clear and technically sound method, thoroughly validated across multiple external standard benchmarks with solid ablations and explicit discussion of failure modes and efficiency trade-offs.

The authors transparently report that patch embedding can introduce flying pixels and that training solely on indoor synthetic data causes blurred boundaries in distant regions [E496d30410c596036-B0039].

    In this paper, we present FlowMoDE, a flow-matching generative model for sim-to-real monocular depth estimation to recover fine structural details. Trained solely on synthetic data, our model outperforms prior methods on five diverse benchmarks. We further validate its strong ability to re- construct fine-grained geometry in various robotic scenarios against state-of-the-art approaches. With fewer Transformer blocks and minimal inference steps, FlowMoDE is lighter and more efficient than existing generative depth models. Still, patch embedding in our flow-matching pipeline may produce occasional flying pixels. Moreover, training only on near-field indoor synthetic data combined with patch-wise denoising can cause blurred edges for distant regions.
