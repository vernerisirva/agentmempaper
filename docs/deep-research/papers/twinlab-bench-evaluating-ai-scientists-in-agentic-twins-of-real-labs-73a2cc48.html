Quality reviewed

The paper provides an exceptionally rigorous, multi-faceted validation of an experimental design benchmark, addressing data contamination, simulation fidelity, judge bias, and exploitability through held-out experiments, counterfactual lab setups, red-teaming, and model-agnostic judge checks. The evidence firmly supports the utility and soundness of the benchmark.

The work candidly documents the minor reliance on local extrapolation outside recorded datasets, checks its sensitivity, audits simulator leakages, and discusses failure modes associated with deterministic boundary evaluations [Ee38663f3900beca1-B0036, Ee38663f3900beca1-B0038, Ee38663f3900beca1-B0089, Ee38663f3900beca1-B0109].

    AI agents are increasingly expected to act as AI scientists, which requires three increasingly open abilities: carrying out a given computational workflow correctly, choosing the next measurements from a given design space, and designing their own experiments. Existing benchmarks mainly evaluate the first two abilities, in a terminal or in a closed loop that selects from predefined candidates. Evaluating the third ability requires reliably executing an experiment that an agent designs, with readings grounded in a real laboratory. We introduceTwinLab-Bench, which evaluates AI scientists on self-designed experiments in 52 tasks from laboratories in the physical sciences, the life sciences, and engineering.     Rubric.The rubric of each task consists of items with conditions for full and partial credit (Starace et al., 2025; Arora et al., 2025). Most items belong to two components. Conclusion items check whether the report states the correct answer, and verification items check whether the report reaches this answer from evidence that the actor agent obtained in this run, so a key finding of a task is often split into one item of each kind. Score and judge.The score of each task is rescaled to 0 to 100, and each task marks a few core items whose failure makes the report wrong; when any core item misses full credit, the run scores 0. We report the mean score over all tasks. The judge agent is a vision-language model that serves as the judge (Zheng et al., 2023), reads the full task, the interaction log, and the report, and cites the report text or the request number for every item.
