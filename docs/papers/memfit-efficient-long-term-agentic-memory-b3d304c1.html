Quality reviewed

The manuscript provides a well-motivated architectural alternative to costly LLM-driven memory consolidation, supported by solid experimental methodology, meaningful baselines, multi-metric evaluations, and comprehensive ablation analyses. The claims are fully aligned with the empirical findings.

The authors clearly acknowledge the inherent trade-off of append-only storage without consolidation, noting that multi-hop synthesis across distant sessions becomes harder at retrieval time. They also document limitations regarding supported modalities and privacy risks associated with verbatim storage.

These limitations raise a central research question: Can a long-term memory system achieve efficient storage and precise retrieval without relying on frequent LLM calls? In this paper, we introduce MemFit, a long-term memory system for conversational agents that addresses this critical question by keeping the LLM out of the write path and out of the search itself.     TextTiling (Hearst, 1997) segments consecutive episodes without an LLM by comparing their embeddings across each gap. At every gap, the cosine similarity between the mean embeddings of the b episodes on either side gives a coherence score, with b= max(3,round(√n/2)) forN stored episodes, and a boundary is placed at each local minimum whose depth below its neighboring peaks exceeds µd +σd/2, whereµd andσd are the mean and standard deviation of the depth over all gaps.     We perform a systematic ablation study on the LoCoMo dataset to quantify the contribution of each core component of the MemFit architecture (Table 3, left). Removing the context window produces the largest degradation, with average F1 falling by 1.86 points and the judge score by 1.7, confirming that a retrieved turn is often not self-contained without its neighbors. Pseudo-relevance feedback, summary expansion, the wide first-stage scan, and the summaries themselves each account for between 0.83 and 1.35 average F1. The components therefore overlap, each recovering evidence the others partly reach, and removing all of them at once leaves plain retrieval, which costs 3.32 average F1 and 5.5 judge points (p<0.001).     E LIMITATIONS While MemFit provides promising results across the three datasets tested, and substantially reduces memory construction cost and time, we acknowledge there are some important limitations. First, due to the fact that MemFit does not rely on LLM consolidation or organization during storage, retrieving and combining memories across sessions becomes a harder task at retrieval time, leading to slightly decreased performance in questions that require these abilities. Additionally, while applying a similar approach as the text-image fusion to other modalities is feasible, the current system only handles text and image modalities. 20 Preprint. Under review.
