Quality reviewed

This work provides a sound, highly transparent, and realistic systems-level evaluation of latency attacks and defenses in real-time object detection, characterized by honest negative findings regarding end-to-end client deadlines and fully documented artifacts.

The authors explicitly report threats to validity, noting that K* calibration was conducted over limited sessions, that millisecond timings are target-specific rather than formal WCET bounds, and that bounding suppression work is insufficient by itself to secure the end-to-end serving pipeline.

Candidate-inflation latency attacks drive the number of boxes entering non-maximum suppres sion (NMS) far beyond its clean range. Recent evaluations report that realistic hardware, represen tations and optimized suppression reduce the severity of such attacks. Reduced severity is not a bo und: work remains attacker-controlled, and prior studies do not jointly report deadline-relative tail la tency and deadline-miss ratio for a defended serving path.
